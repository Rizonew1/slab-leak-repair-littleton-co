Columbine West, west of Pierce Street near Clement Park, is a neighborhood of 1970s-era homes. Many have basements or garage slabs with copper lines beneath — and many are now on their second or third owner, who may not know the plumbing history.

What plumbing issues do Columbine West homes have?

Homes here were built in a fairly short window, so they tend to share similar plumbing layouts. That's useful: once we've found where lines typically run in a given floor plan, detection goes faster.

  • Copper supply lines under basement slabs, around 50 years old
  • Lines entering through the garage slab near the water heater
  • Basement bathrooms added later, sometimes with mixed materials
  • Hot lines that have worked hardest and fail first

What new owners in Columbine West should check

If you've recently bought here, ask whether the home has had slab leaks or a repipe — it's often in the inspection report or the seller's disclosure. A quick water-meter check (all fixtures off, watch the dial) is a free way to catch a hidden leak early.

Many homes near Clement Park have finished basements with carpet over the slab. That's good news for repairs: carpet can be pulled back and re-stretched, so a spot repair is often practical if the pipe around the leak looks sound.

If water ever surfaces through the floor, shut the main valve — usually in the basement near where the line enters — and call our 24/7 emergency line.

What we check first in a Columbine West home

We check the meter, the water heater and the laundry area first, because in many homes of this era those three sit close together and the supply lines branch from there under the basement slab. We also look for a basement bathroom added after the original build — those were sometimes plumbed with a mix of materials and joined in ways that fail sooner. Knowing those layouts means detection is often quicker here, which keeps your bill down.

Tip for Columbine West homeowners: check the age of your washer hoses. Burst rubber hoses are a common cause of basement water that gets mistaken for a slab leak, and braided steel hoses are an easy upgrade.

How do we repair slab leaks in Columbine West?

  1. Call and triage. Tell us what you're seeing; we'll give you emergency steps if water is surfacing.
  2. Confirm and isolate. A meter check and pressure test show whether the leak is on the hot, cold or drain side.
  3. Pinpoint. We mark the leak on your floor, usually to within about a foot.
  4. Choose a repair. For homes with 1970s tract homes with basement and garage slabs, we'll explain which option fits — spot repair, reroute, lining or repipe — with written prices.
  5. Repair, test and restore. Work areas are contained, the repair is pressure-tested, and the floor or walls are restored.

Signs of a slab leak to watch for

  • Water meter moving with every fixture off
  • Warm patch on the floor, or the water heater running nonstop
  • Damp carpet, musty smell or lifting floors on a lower level
  • Sound of running water when nothing is on
  • Unexplained jump in the water or gas bill

Columbine West slab leak FAQ

I just bought a home in Columbine West. How do I check for a slab leak?

Turn everything off and watch the meter's leak dial for 10–15 minutes. If it moves, call us for detection.

Are basement-carpet repairs cheaper?

Usually. Carpet can be pulled back and reinstalled, unlike tile or hardwood.

Do you know the typical pipe layout in these homes?

Homes built in the same era often share layouts, which can make detection faster, but we always confirm with testing.

What if the leak is under a finished basement bedroom?

We pinpoint it tightly and often reroute through the ceiling to avoid opening carpet and built-ins.
