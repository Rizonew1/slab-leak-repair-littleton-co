Do newer Grant Ranch homes get slab leaks?

Yes — less often than 50-year-old homes, but they do. Newer homes usually have PEX or copper supply lines, and when they leak under a slab, the cause tends to be different:

  • Installation damage, such as a line nicked or kinked when the slab was poured
  • Fittings under the slab that fail over time
  • Ground movement in clay soil stressing lines at the foundation
  • Lines that run close to the edge of a slab and see temperature swings

Detecting leaks in newer homes

Because many newer homes use PEX, detection relies more on pressure isolation and thermal imaging than on acoustic listening alone. Tracer gas can help where needed.

Repairs in newer homes are often simpler: if the line is newer and the leak is a single defect, a spot repair is a reasonable choice, especially under carpet or in a garage. If the leak is under a large tile or wood floor, a short reroute can avoid tearing it up.

Some newer homes still carry builder or home warranties. Check whether yours covers plumbing before the repair — we provide the documentation you'd need.

What we check first in a Grant Ranch home

In newer homes, we look closely at the manifold or main distribution point and the first few feet where lines enter the slab, since fittings and installation damage tend to show up there. We review whether the home has had any settlement or cracking that could point to ground movement. Because many homes here are still relatively new, we also make sure you know whether any warranty may apply before work begins.

Tip for Grant Ranch homeowners: if your home has a manifold, make sure each valve is labeled. In a leak, shutting off one circuit instead of the whole house keeps the rest of your home running normally.

How do we repair slab leaks in Grant Ranch?

  1. Call and triage. Tell us what you're seeing; we'll give you emergency steps if water is surfacing.
  2. Confirm and isolate. A meter check and pressure test show whether the leak is on the hot, cold or drain side.
  3. Pinpoint. We mark the leak on your floor, usually to within about a foot.
  4. Choose a repair. For homes with late 1990s–2000s homes around Grant Ranch's lakes, we'll explain which option fits — spot repair, reroute, lining or repipe — with written prices.
  5. Repair, test and restore. Work areas are contained, the repair is pressure-tested, and the floor or walls are restored.

Signs of a slab leak to watch for

  • Water meter moving with every fixture off
  • Warm patch on the floor, or the water heater running nonstop
  • Damp carpet, musty smell or lifting floors on a lower level
  • Sound of running water when nothing is on
  • Unexplained jump in the water or gas bill
Emergency tip: If water is surfacing through the floor in your Grant Ranch home, shut off the main valve and the water heater, then call us right away. We're open 24/7.

Grant Ranch slab leak FAQ

My Grant Ranch home is only 20–25 years old. Can it really have a slab leak?

Yes. Installation defects, fittings and ground movement can cause leaks in newer homes too.

Is PEX harder to find leaks in?

It's quieter than copper, so we combine pressure isolation, thermal imaging and tracer gas.

Will a home warranty cover this?

Some do, partially. We document the leak to help with any claim.

Do lake-area homes have higher slab leak risk?

Water tables and soil moisture vary locally. We check drainage around the home as part of diagnosis.
