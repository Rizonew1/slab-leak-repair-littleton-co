Epoxy pipe lining repairs a leaking pipe from the inside. The line is dried, cleaned of corrosion with an abrasive, and coated with epoxy that cures into a smooth, sealed layer. Pinholes are filled, and the inner wall is protected from further corrosion. Because the work is done through openings at the ends of the run, the slab usually stays intact.

How does epoxy pipe lining work?

  1. Diagnose with detection and, for drains, camera inspection to confirm the pipe is a candidate.
  2. Isolate the line and drain it.
  3. Dry the pipe with heated, filtered air.
  4. Clean the inside with an abrasive blast that removes scale and corrosion down to sound metal.
  5. Coat by blowing or pulling epoxy through the line until the full inner wall is covered.
  6. Cure the epoxy, then pressure-test.
  7. Reconnect fixtures and restore water.

Is lining right for your pipes?

Lining is a good match in some situations and the wrong one in others. Honest screening matters.

Good candidatePoor candidate
Pinhole leaks from internal corrosionPipe crushed, separated or badly misaligned
Copper that is pitted but structurally soundPipe already lined or heavily patched
Lines under expensive floors or cabinetsVery small or very long runs with many fittings (check case-by-case)
Homes where opening walls is hardWhen a reroute is cheaper and simpler

Lining for drains under the slab

A related method — cured-in-place lining — repairs cracked cast-iron and ABS drains under the slab. We cover that on our under-slab sewer line page.

How long does lining last?

Manufacturers commonly publish multi-decade service-life estimates for properly applied epoxy liners. Results depend on how well the pipe is cleaned and coated, which is why preparation is most of the job.

Why do slab leaks happen in Littleton homes?

Littleton's housing stock tells most of the story. A large share of homes went up during the building boom of the 1960s through the 1980s, and many of them have copper water lines buried in or under a concrete floor — a basement slab, a garage slab, or the slab of a ranch or split-level that sits on grade. Newer subdivisions on the south and west sides were often plumbed with PEX, which fails differently but can still be damaged under concrete.

  • Expansive clay soil. Much of the Front Range sits on clay that swells when wet and shrinks when dry. That seasonal movement pushes and pulls on pipes locked in concrete, and fittings and bends take the strain first.
  • Abrasion. A copper line that rests against gravel, rebar or the edge of the slab vibrates every time water flows. Over decades it rubs a hole through its own wall.
  • Water chemistry and age. Pinhole corrosion from the inside is common in older copper. Once one pinhole appears, others along the same run often follow.
  • Freeze-thaw and construction defects. Lines that run near an exterior wall, a crawl-space transition or a poorly bedded trench are more exposed to stress.

Why preparation decides how long lining lasts

An epoxy liner can only bond to clean, dry pipe. If scale, moisture or debris is left inside, the coating can thin out or fail to bond in spots. That's why most of a lining job is preparation: drying the pipe completely, then blasting the inside until bare metal shows. We inspect before coating and test after curing.

Is lining right for Littleton homes?

Lining fits best in Littleton homes with sound but corroding copper under finished floors that would be expensive to open, and in townhomes and condos where opening a shared slab is complicated. In homes with easy reroute paths — like ranches with open basement ceilings — a reroute is often the simpler and cheaper choice. We price both when both are realistic.

What to expect on lining day

  1. Water is shut off to the lines being lined; other fixtures may stay on.
  2. Fixtures and valves on the run are disconnected and protected.
  3. Drying and cleaning equipment runs for several hours — it's noisy but contained.
  4. Coating and cure take the rest of the day for most single runs.
  5. Fixtures are reconnected and the line is tested before water is restored.
Do you need to open walls for lining?

Usually only small access points at the ends of the run — far less than a reroute.

Epoxy lining for townhomes and condos

In attached homes, opening a slab can affect a neighbor's unit, and HOA rules may limit what can be done to shared structure. Lining from inside your own unit avoids most of that. It's one of the main reasons we see lining requested in Littleton's townhome communities near the riverfront and along major corridors.

Questions to ask about any lining job

  • What product is used, and is it rated for drinking water?
  • How will the pipe be cleaned, and how is that verified?
  • How is the cure confirmed before water is restored?
  • What happens if a section can't be lined?
  • Is there written coverage for the work, and what does it include?

How long does lining take compared to other repairs?

Most single-run lining jobs take one day, sometimes two for complex runs. Whole-home lining takes longer and is usually done in stages so you keep water for part of each day. Compare that to a spot repair, which needs concrete cure time before flooring, or a reroute, which needs drywall patching afterward.

Can lining be combined with other repairs?

Yes. A common approach in Littleton homes is to reroute the hot line, which fails most often, and line a cold run that's harder to reach. Mixing methods lets us choose the best option for each run instead of forcing one approach on the whole house.

Who is lining not for?

If your pipes are already failing in several ways — crushed sections, separated joints, many repairs — or if you're planning a remodel that opens walls anyway, a repipe is usually the better investment. Lining shines when you want to keep floors and walls intact and the pipe is still structurally sound. We'll tell you plainly which group your home falls into after testing, and we'll price the alternatives side by side.

Epoxy lining FAQ

Is epoxy lining safe for drinking water?

Epoxies used for potable water lines are certified for that use. We use products rated for drinking water and confirm cure before restoring service.

Can you line PEX?

Lining is mostly used for metal pipe. PEX leaks are usually handled with a reroute or spot repair.

Will lining reduce my water pressure?

The coating is thin. Removing corrosion during cleaning often offsets any change, and many homes see similar or better flow.

How long is the house without water?

Usually a day or less per line being lined.

Does every slab leak qualify for lining?

No. We inspect and test first, and if lining isn't a good fit we tell you and explain the alternatives.

Can lining fix a leak that's already pouring water?

Lining seals pinholes, but an active large break may need a spot repair first. We'll assess on site.

How do I know lining worked?

We pressure-test after cure and can show you the readings before water is restored.

Is lining more expensive than a reroute?

Often it is for a single run, but it can be cheaper when the alternative means opening tile, hardwood or a shared slab.
