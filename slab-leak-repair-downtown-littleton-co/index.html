What kind of plumbing do downtown Littleton homes have?

The streets around Main Street and the Littleton/Downtown light rail station hold some of the oldest housing in the city. Many original homes have basements or crawl spaces rather than slabs, but over the decades many were remodeled — additions poured on slab, basements finished, garages converted. Those slab-on-grade additions are where we often find leaks: a supply line run under a new concrete floor during a remodel, sometimes decades ago, with whatever material the contractor had on hand.

Newer townhomes and infill builds near the downtown core are typically slab-on-grade or have basement slabs, often plumbed with PEX or copper under the floor.

  • Remodel-era copper lines under addition slabs
  • Transitions between old galvanized steel and copper that corrode at the joint
  • Older cast-iron drains under basement floors
  • PEX in newer infill townhomes, sometimes damaged during construction

Why slab leaks in older downtown homes need extra care

Older homes carry surprises. A home that has been remodeled several times may have pipe that runs nowhere near where you'd expect. That makes line tracing and pressure isolation especially important before we recommend opening any concrete.

Many downtown homes also have original hardwood, vintage tile or finished basements that owners want to protect. When the leak sits under something you can't replace easily, rerouting the line overhead or through a basement ceiling often wins over breaking the floor.

We're based on S Sycamore St, close to the historic downtown area, so a call from this part of town is a short drive for our technicians.

What we check first in a downtown Littleton home

In older homes we start by mapping where the water actually goes. We find the main, follow it to the water heater and fixtures, and note every material change — galvanized to copper, copper to PEX — because those transitions are where trouble hides. We also look at whether a basement or addition slab was poured over existing lines, which tells us where a leak is likely. That groundwork means we only open concrete when we're confident of the spot, which matters a lot in homes with original floors homeowners want to keep.

Tip for downtown homeowners: keep remodel records with the house. Knowing when an addition was poured and who plumbed it can save hours of detection.

How do we repair slab leaks in Historic Downtown Littleton?

  1. Call and triage. Tell us what you're seeing; we'll give you emergency steps if water is surfacing.
  2. Confirm and isolate. A meter check and pressure test show whether the leak is on the hot, cold or drain side.
  3. Pinpoint. We mark the leak on your floor, usually to within about a foot.
  4. Choose a repair. For homes with early 1900s cottages, mid-century bungalows and infill builds, we'll explain which option fits — spot repair, reroute, lining or repipe — with written prices.
  5. Repair, test and restore. Work areas are contained, the repair is pressure-tested, and the floor or walls are restored.

Signs of a slab leak to watch for

  • Water meter moving with every fixture off
  • Warm patch on the floor, or the water heater running nonstop
  • Damp carpet, musty smell or lifting floors on a lower level
  • Sound of running water when nothing is on
  • Unexplained jump in the water or gas bill

Historic Downtown Littleton slab leak FAQ

Do older downtown homes have slab leaks if they have basements?

Yes. Basement floors are concrete slabs too, and lines often run beneath them. Slab-on-grade additions are another common spot.

Can you work around original hardwood or tile?

Usually. We favor reroutes or lining when the floor over the leak is hard to replace, and we explain the trade-offs.

Do you see galvanized pipe downtown?

Sometimes, particularly in older homes. Where galvanized connects to copper, the joint can corrode and leak. We check during detection.

Is it worth repiping an older downtown home?

If the supply lines are a mix of old materials and you've had leaks, a repipe often makes sense. We'll price it alongside a single repair.
